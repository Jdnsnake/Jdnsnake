FAQ

Common questions

What happens if I forget to say "Uno"?

If you play your second-to-last card and forget to say "Uno!" before the next player takes their turn, you must draw 2 penalty cards when caught.

Can I stack Draw Two cards?

In official rules, no — you cannot stack +2 on +2. The next player must draw 2 and skip. House rules often allow stacking though.

What if the deck runs out?

Shuffle the discard pile (except the top card) to form a new draw pile and continue.

Who wins if two players go out at the same time?

This can't happen — only one player goes at a time. The first player to play their last card wins immediately.

The rules

Exact landing to win

You must hit square 100 exactly. Overshoot and bounce back by the remainder.

One die, standard play

Standard play uses a single six-sided die. Some editions use two — check your box.

Sharing squares is fine

Multiple players can occupy the same square. Nobody gets knocked out.

Heads & feet only

Snakes activate on the head only. Ladders activate at the foot only. Landing on a body section does nothing.

FAQ

Common questions

Do I need a specific roll to start?

In standard play, no. Your token starts off the board and your first roll brings it on: roll a 3 and you land on square 3. Some editions require a 6 to enter; check your board's instructions.

What if I roll more than needed to reach 100?

You move to 100 then bounce back by the leftover squares. On 97 with a roll of 5: go to 100, back 2 — you land on 98.

Can two players share a square?

Yes, multiple tokens can sit on the same square. Nobody is knocked out in standard rules.

What if I land on a snake's body or a ladder's middle?

Nothing happens. Snakes only activate at the head; ladders only at the foot.

Is there any skill, or is it pure luck?

Pure luck — the dice decides everything. That's the charm: anyone can win, making it great for all ages.

How many players can play?

Most boards support 2–6 comfortably. Above 6 the game slows down, but there's no hard limit.

How to play Chess

Eleven short steps from complete beginner to your first game, following the official FIDE Laws of Chess.

The Pieces

How each piece moves

Six piece types, each with its own move. No piece may land on a square occupied by one of its own side. Point values are a common guide, not a rule.

♚︎

King

One square in any direction, or castles. May never move to a square attacked by an enemy piece. (Art. 3.8)

Priceless: protect it
♛︎

Queen

Any number of squares along a rank, file or diagonal. Can't jump over pieces. (Art. 3.4–3.5)

9 points
♜︎

Rook

Any number of squares along a rank or file. Can't jump over pieces. (Art. 3.3, 3.5)

5 points
♝︎

Bishop

Any number of squares along a diagonal, so it always stays on its starting colour. Can't jump over pieces. (Art. 3.2, 3.5)

3 points
♞︎

Knight

Moves in an L: two squares one way, then one square to the side. The only piece that jumps over others. (Art. 3.6)

3 points
♟︎

Pawn

One square straight forward, or two on its first move. Captures one square diagonally forward. Never moves backwards. (Art. 3.7)

1 point
Key Rules

Essential rules to know

Board orientation

The corner square on each player's right must be light: "light on the right". The white queen starts on d1 (light), the black queen on d8 (dark).FIDE Laws Art. 2.1–2.3

Check

Your king is attacked. You must deal with it straight away by moving the king, blocking, or capturing the attacker. No move may leave your own king in check.FIDE Laws Art. 3.9

Checkmate

The king is in check and there is no legal move to escape. The player who delivers checkmate wins, and the game ends immediately.FIDE Laws Art. 1.4, 5.1.1

Castling

King moves two squares towards a rook; the rook jumps to the square the king crossed (O-O: Kg1 Rf1, O-O-O: Kc1 Rd1). Not allowed if the king or that rook has moved, a square between them is occupied, or the king is in check, crosses an attacked square or lands in check.FIDE Laws Art. 3.8.2

En passant

When an enemy pawn advances two squares and lands beside your pawn, you may capture it as if it had moved one square, but only on your very next move.FIDE Laws Art. 3.7.3.1–3.7.3.2

Promotion

A pawn reaching the far rank must become a queen, rook, bishop or knight of the same colour, as part of the same move. Your choice isn't limited to pieces you've lost.FIDE Laws Art. 3.7.3.3–3.7.3.5

Stalemate and dead positions

If the player to move has no legal move and is not in check, it's stalemate: an immediate draw. A position where neither side can ever checkmate (e.g. king against king) is also an immediate draw.FIDE Laws Art. 5.2.1–5.2.2

Other draws

Players may agree a draw once both have moved. A player may claim a draw when the same position occurs three times, or after 50 moves each with no capture or pawn move. At five repetitions or 75 such moves the game is drawn automatically.FIDE Laws Art. 5.2.3, 9.2, 9.3, 9.6

Resigning

A player may resign at any time, and the opponent wins. The one exception: if the opponent could never checkmate by any series of legal moves, the game is a draw instead.FIDE Laws Art. 5.1.2

Reading the move log

Moves are written in algebraic notation: piece letter (K, Q, R, B, N; none for a pawn) plus the square, e.g. Nf3 or e4. x = capture, + = check, # = checkmate, O-O / O-O-O = castling, e8=Q = promotion, e.p. = en passant.FIDE Laws Appendix C

FAQ

Common questions

Who goes first?

White always makes the first move, then the players take turns (FIDE Art. 1.2). White scores slightly better in practice because of the extra tempo.

Can the king capture pieces?

Yes, if the enemy piece is on an adjoining square and isn't protected. The king can never move to a square attacked by an enemy piece, so it can't take a protected piece (Art. 3.8.1, 3.9.2).

What if I have no legal moves?

If your king is in check, it's checkmate and you lose. If it isn't in check, it's stalemate and the game is drawn immediately, whatever the material (Art. 5.2.1).

Can I castle out of check, or through check?

No. You can't castle while in check, the king can't cross a square attacked by an enemy piece, and it can't land in check. The rook may pass over an attacked square; only the king's path matters (Art. 3.8.2.2).

How does the 50-move rule work?

If each player has made 50 moves with no capture and no pawn move, the player to move may claim a draw (Art. 9.3). After 75 moves each it's drawn automatically (Art. 9.6.2). In our game, a "Claim draw" button appears when you're entitled to claim.

What is threefold repetition?

If the same position (same player to move, same pieces on the same squares, same castling and en passant options) occurs three times, the player to move may claim a draw. At five times the game is drawn automatically (Art. 9.2, 9.6.1).

What is "touch-move"?

Over the board, if you deliberately touch one of your pieces you must move it, and if you touch an opponent's piece you must capture it, if legally possible. Say "I adjust" first if you only want to straighten a piece (Art. 4.2–4.3). Touch-move doesn't apply in our game: you click a piece, then click the square to move it to. Until you click a square, you can click a different piece (or the same piece again) to change your mind.

Can I have two queens?

Yes. When a pawn promotes you may choose a queen, rook, bishop or knight, and your choice is not limited to pieces that have been captured (Art. 3.7.3.4).

The No Mercy rules

🃏 Draw until you can play

If you can't match the top card, you must keep drawing until you draw a card you can play, then play it. The rest of the cards you drew stay in your hand.

📚 Stacking

When someone plays a draw card (+2, +4, +6, +10) you can stack by playing a draw card of equal or higher value; colour doesn't matter. The first player who can't (or won't) stack takes the whole total and loses their turn.

7️⃣ Sevens swap

When you play a 7, you must swap your hand with another player of your choice.

0️⃣ Zeros pass

When you play a 0, every player passes their hand to the next player in the current direction.

💀 The Mercy rule

If a player ever has 25 or more cards, they're out of the game. Their cards are set aside until the draw pile runs out, then shuffled back in.

📢 Call UNO

The moment you're down to 1 card, call "UNO!". If another player catches you first, you draw 2.

🚩 Starting card

If the first card flipped is an action or wild card, it's ignored and the next card is flipped.

🏆 Winning

Play your last card first, or be the last player left after everyone else is knocked out by the Mercy rule.

Scoring (optional)

The round winner scores the cards left in the other players' hands, plus a bonus for everyone knocked out. First to 1,000 points wins the game.

FAQ

Common questions

Can I stack a +2 on a +4?

No. You can only stack a draw card of equal or higher value: +2 on +2, +4 on +2 or +4, +6 on anything up to +6, and +10 on anything. A Wild Reverse Draw 4 counts as +4.

Does colour matter when stacking?

No. When you stack, you ignore the colour of the previous card, so a green +4 can go on a yellow +2.

Who picks the colour for Colour Roulette?

The next player (the victim) picks the colour. They flip cards until that colour appears and keep every card flipped. That colour becomes the colour in play, and they lose their turn.

Do I have to play the card I drew?

Yes. When you can't play, you keep drawing until you draw a playable card, and then you must play it.

What if I play a 7 as my last card?

You've played your last card, so you win before any swap happens.

What happens to a knocked-out player's cards?

They're set aside. When the draw pile runs out, they're shuffled back in with the discard pile.
